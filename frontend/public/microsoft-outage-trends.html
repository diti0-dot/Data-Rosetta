Trending Websites Across Europe During the Microsoft Outage

The websites that were trending across Europe during the Microsoft 365 outage, November 12–13, 2026.

Note on timing. The outage began at 5 p.m. Dutch time on 12 November, after most of the European working day. As a result, data for 13 November likely reflects the outage period in Europe more closely.


These are websites that saw an increase in popularity in Cloudflare's data during the selected date. They are not necessarily the most visited websites. The ranking shows which websites were gaining attention, not how many people visited them.

Which domains experienced rising popularity during the outage observation period?

On 12 November 2026, a Microsoft 365 outage disrupted several services, primarily Exchange Online, including Outlook, Teams and Copilot. Users were unable to send or receive email or access their inboxes, and the disruption extended into the following workday across Europe. Microsoft’s Service Health recorded the incident as lasting 30 hours. Because so many workplaces rely on these tools, the outage affected normal working patterns for a large share of users.

This analysis looks at which domains gained popularity across Europe during the outage observation period of 12–13 November 2026. It uses Cloudflare’s trending-domain data, which ranks domains by how much their popularity rank rose in each country. The map shows the top trending domain and its category for each country, and selecting a country shows its full top 10 for the chosen date.

12 November. On the first day, the top trending domains varied widely by country and category. Job search sites led in Ireland, Andorra and Cyprus. Educational institutions ranked highly in Spain and Poland. Information security and remote-access tools led in Monaco. Ecommerce, clothing and television sites led in countries such as Czechia, the United Kingdom and Bulgaria.

13 November. On the second day, the results were more uniform. The YouTube analytics tool vidiq.com was the top trending domain in 11 countries. Gaming domains led in Estonia and Latvia, while Spain and Türkiye were led by finance and television sites.

Denmark. One notable result is meesho.com, an Indian ecommerce marketplace, which was Denmark’s top trending domain on 12 November. Its rise does not have a clear explanation from this data alone. The trending metric measures change in rank rather than total traffic, so a domain with little traffic can rise sharply from a relatively small increase in visits.
